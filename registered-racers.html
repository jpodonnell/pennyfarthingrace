Eric Norris

Columbus, OH
Riding Since

Bike
Standard High Wheel Tourer
Front Wheel
54"
How they got into penny farthing riding

My favorite place to ride my high wheel is in parades — I've ridden the Bexley, Ohio 4th of July Parade for 54 years, plus the Memorial Day Parade, the Pro Football Hall of Fame Parade, the Cleveland Saint Patrick's Day Parade with The Wheelmen, and multiple Rides of Silence. I also ride Columbus's many paved trails. The bikes themselves inspire me — no flats, no chains, no derailleurs to worry about, just pedal on peacefully.

Fun fact

Retired from Nationwide Insurance, and cycling of all sorts is now my playtime. My favorite achievement is learning new tricks while riding in the Bexley Parade — some invented, some borrowed from fellow performers — and even more rewarding, my son-in-law and now my grandson ride and do tricks with me in the parade too. I've been riding bikes my whole life, racing skills competitions, criteriums, cyclocross, mountain bike, and winter fat bike races, with thousands of road miles — including 34 years (and counting) on the 210-mile, two-day Tour of the Scioto River (TOSRV).

How they got into penny farthing riding

After watching the National Clustered Spires Highwheel Race in Frederick, MD, several times over the years, I took a lesson from another highwheeler. I was instantly hooked and bought a Rideable Bike Replica (RBR) Excelsior Standard 52″ the next day! After three races with a heavier leisure-intended bike, I sought out a Standard High Wheel Racer and upped to 54″. I ride both bikes regularly depending on the type of outing.

Fun fact

I thoroughly enjoy celebrating my German heritage through travel to Oktoberfest in Munich and similar local festivals. I was once dubbed the "Bavarian Doughboy" for my love of pretzels and German bier. Whereas many folks wear old-timey attire in races, I've often sported my lederhosen.

How they got into penny farthing riding

I rediscovered cycling as an adult in the Spring of 2007 while working at Kennedy Space Center. In the years following, I would occasionally see YouTube videos of people riding Penny Farthings and would think, "Wow, that looks like fun!" But it would be 2018 before I got up the courage to order my bicycle from Per-Olaf Kippel in Sweden. Then, in 2022, I made my first race in Frederick, Maryland. And haven't looked back.

Fun fact

Well, it wasn't fun at the time, but four days after bringing home my bike I messed up a dismount and busted two of my right ribs. Two weeks later I was back in the saddle and thought it would be funny to name my penny, Tumbleweed. Guess the bike didn't like the name because three weeks after that it threw me the other way and I broke my left femur. Ouch! But from the start my wife, Karin, had called the bike "Speedy" — a little pretentious, but safer.

How they got into penny farthing riding

I got into penny farthing riding after watching the exhilarating National Clustered Spires High Wheel Races, which immediately convinced me I had to try it myself. In 2018, after a quick lesson from legendary racer Larry Black, I was riding a high wheel on public roads, and just three months later I showed up at the starting line on my own 48" black Standard Highwheels Tourer. What began as a thrill quickly became a passion, drawing me into a rare, welcoming global community of adventurous riders, and eventually leading my wife and me to race and ride in places like Sweden, Tasmania, and across the US — turning high wheel riding into an unforgettable way of life rather than just a hobby.

Fun fact

When I'm not working as a staff research scientist, I teach riding high wheel bicycles — I've even ridden through the historic streets of Old San Juan, Puerto Rico alongside premier international racer Fidel Irizarry to help raise funds for a local cancer treatment center.

It took 4 years of watching the high wheel race in my adopted hometown of Frederick to go from mesmerized to "can anyone do that?" to "I'm going to buy one."
